The weather is getting weird. Turntide Islands is a community of people who
would rather respond to that than merely react. We're gardeners, tinkerers,
researchers, organizers, and the curious, swapping practical, local knowledge
about living well through it: what to plant, how to drain a yard, which
sources to trust, and how to look after each other.

Useful knowledge about adaptation is scattered, hard to check, and rarely
paid for. So we keep receipts. A good conversation gets rescued into the
Knowledge Vault, evidence travels with the claims it supports, and the
community's decisions are written down where anyone can read them. This site
is where that public record is kept.

# Start with a question and its evidence

[[rain-garden-question|Where could a rain garden help?]] brings together
public guidance, measured results from other places, and the facts a local
answer still needs.

The [[duluth-garden-drainage|Duluth garden record]] follows one public garden
marker into a watershed map, a terrain map with downloadable data, and NOAA
precipitation estimates with their uncertainty. You can read the sources and
reuse the data here without a community account or a paid report.

These are AI-prepared desktop records. They do not establish garden drainage,
site suitability, or a completed local trial. The records name what is known,
what remains unresolved, and how someone can contribute evidence.

# Come ashore

The islands are on Discord. [[join|Join Turntide Islands]], and you'll land
at `#dock`, our harbor. Discord's onboarding there asks what brought you and
which islands you'd like to visit, and a person welcomes you in. After that,
**Channels & Roles** at the top of the channel list is where you change which
islands you visit.

Not sure where you'd fit? Look over the [[islands|chart of the islands]]
first: the Mainland for introductions, questions, and field notes, the
Chatter Isles for gardens, cooking, climate, scholarship, tech, and markets,
the Knowledge Vault for evidence and our own news wire, and Governance Rock
for how we run ourselves.
